    Vision Lab chapter 1 rebuild and chapter 2 migration¶

    Status: implemented in the playable course. The chapter map sets the teaching sequence and the runtime policy. Lecture references below use the local PDF page order and must be confirmed against the PDFs, which are not committed. The machine scenarios, test values and acceptance rules are design fixtures.

    Target and sequence¶

    A learner investigates a blur, builds a spatial processor, adds an efficient form for kernels that factor, and uses it to find the boundary of a noisy grayscale scan. Old 1-9 leaves this chapter; trainable channel aggregation belongs to chapter 4. Resolve every reference to the retired lesson and its part in the content operation that removes it.

    Level IDs follow play order. Saves are not migrated during development, so the renumbering has no player cost:

    New Old Lesson Processor
    1-1 1-1 Probe a blur, then weight neighbours —
    1-2 1-3 General weighted kernel publishes the dense form
    1-3 1-4 Convolution direction upgrades the dense form
    1-4 1-6 Finite boundaries upgrades the dense form
    1-5 1-2 Two passes when the kernel factors adds the separable form
    1-6 1-5 Smooth and enhance calls
    1-7 1-7 Signed directional response calls
    1-8 1-8 Sobel and response strength calls
    1-9 1-10 Grayscale scan calls

    The renames form a cycle (1-2 → 1-5 → 1-6 → 1-4 → 1-3 → 1-2). Remove old 1-9 first, then apply the renames through temporary IDs in one changeset. Task IDs (v1_*) are already distinct and keep their values.

    Chapter layout: 1-1 → 1-2 → 1-3 → 1-4 → 1-5, then a parallel group with the smoothing route [1-6] and the response route [1-7, 1-8], joining at 1-9. Neither route uses the other's results; the scanner uses both.

    Only 1-2 to 1-5 publish the spatial processor. 1-6 to 1-9 are application boards that call it. Remove builds from old 1-7, 1-8 and 1-10, which currently publish processor revisions. The scanner's threshold and response outputs belong to the scanner level. Chapter 2 calls the dense form.

    Level specifications¶

    In the table, a starter gives tools, supplied signals and reusable context. The reference board and the rejected approaches belong to the level checks. The player sees causal feedback from the rejected approach, with the general formula in the completion text and linked encyclopedia.

    Level and decision Starter and player action Pass evidence and counterexample Source
    1-1 · Probe a blur, then weight neighbours. Which input reveals where the output comes from? Add the opening probe below, then keep the current signed three-point construction and vertical transfer. The probe supplies no reusable part or board to the construction tasks. Probe: the evidence predicate below. Construction: a displaced impulse, a constant with non-unit weight sum and a vertical case. A circuit using only the centre passes some constant examples and fails the impulse. L3 pp. 9–18, 24–28.
    1-2 · General weighted kernel. How does one neighbourhood become one output? Supply window expansion, the written kernel and shape restoration. The player connects the contribution and reduction paths, then generalises across the image. Publish the dense form. Distinct 3×3 and 5×3 kernels on a non-square image, a signed kernel and a kernel whose sum differs from one. An extra mean or a transposed result fails. L3 pp. 9–18, 32.
    1-3 · Convolution direction. What changes when the kernel is reversed? Carry 1-2. Provide an asymmetric kernel and an off-centre impulse. The player uses a displaced impulse to infer how to reproduce the mathematical convention. Exact orientation and origin, including a rectangular odd kernel. A symmetric Gaussian is an equivalence control. A direct correlation answer fails the asymmetric case. L3 pp. 17–18.
    1-4 · Finite boundaries. Where can the circuit read at the edges? Carry 1-3. Use observed edge traces to select the boundary rule. Expose the shared zero, clamp, wrap and reflect setting through the processor. Keep valid output as an explicit interior crop. A radius-two kernel distinguishes clamp from reflection including the edge; an asymmetric edge signal distinguishes wrap from zero. Exact valid crop and dimensions. A constant interior alone cannot distinguish the policies. L3 pp. 41–45.
    1-5 · Two passes when the kernel factors. When can the same operator use fewer products? Construction task: build the separable form from the same window, multiply, reduce and restore parts, applied with a 1×kx window and then a ky×1 window. Compare against the dense form on the same input in the final task. Final task: an application board with three kernels, a multiply budget and the processor in both forms. The player decides per kernel which form to use and, for a factorable kernel, writes its two factors as constants. Construction: outputs equal the dense form for 3×3, 5×5 and 5×3 outer products with unequal and signed factors, a zero factor, and each boundary rule. Summing two one-axis results, or applying ky along x, fails. Budget: a 5×5 box, a 5×5 Gaussian and a 3×3 Laplacian (rank two), with a budget of 29·W·H products (10 + 10 + 9). Dense everywhere (59·W·H) exceeds it; a separable attempt on the Laplacian gives a wrong output, and writing it as a sum of two separable passes (12·W·H) is correct but over budget. L3 pp. 38–40.
    1-6 · Smooth and enhance. Which signal was removed by smoothing? Give the processor, one-dimensional Gaussian weights, a noisy grayscale image and a gain input. Reuse the three existing tasks on smoothing, residual and enhancement. A constant, a bright and a dark impulse, and gain zero. Outputs are the smoothed image, signed detail, enhanced image and absolute change, as applicable per task. Using absolute detail in enhancement fails the dark case. L3 pp. 26–28, 32, 35.
    1-7 · Signed directional response. Which direction and sign describe an edge? Give the processor, horizontal and vertical difference kernels, and a non-square ramp. The player builds both response paths in one application circuit. Horizontal and vertical ramps and reversed contrast. Using the same kernel for both directions or discarding the sign fails. L3 pp. 29–30 and L5 pp. 39–42.
    1-8 · Sobel and response strength. What does perpendicular smoothing add? Carry the 1-7 board. Provide smoothing weights and an edge whose response fluctuates under interference. The learner tests which use of the weights stabilises the response while preserving the edge. Axis-specific edge fixtures, contrast reversal and a noisy step: numeric response, sign and joint magnitude. Summing signed responses or smoothing along the derivative axis fails. L3 pp. 29–30 and L5 pp. 39–42.
    1-9 · Grayscale scan. How do preprocessing and a threshold interact? Start from the 1-8 board. Supply grayscale scans, an editable Gaussian scale and a threshold. The learner selects a scale by comparing false marks on speckled paper and missed marks on fine strokes. Clean and noisy edges, an edge touching the boundary and a value equal to the threshold. Expect the response and a strict-exceedance mask. Keep a non-judged scan sample that reveals detail loss. L3 pp. 26–30, 41–45 and L5 pp. 39–42. The scan and threshold are application design.

    Opening probe contract¶

    • The local blur is a 3×3 box with unit sum. It accepts the learner's editable array and shows the output as image and numeric values. Provide reset and optional example inputs. Keep submitted evidence separate from the current edit.
    • Two independently judged questions. Support: the candidates are 1×3, 3×1 and 3×3 boxes, all with unit DC gain. Gain: the candidates share the true support and differ in their sums. The learner selects or constructs an input, runs it, and identifies the supported conclusion.
    • Compute every candidate's output on the submitted input with the same boundary rule. The evidence is sufficient when the observed output differs from each wrong candidate beyond tolerance on the inspection domain. A flat input cannot separate the support candidates; an all-zero input cannot separate the gain candidates. Any input meeting the predicate passes: an impulse reveals both support and gain (the output sums to the kernel sum), and a flat interior patch reveals gain directly. The completion text names both.
    • The learner first sees the ordinary output. The neighbour-contribution display is available for inspecting the explanation and reappears with the learner circuit in 1-2. The black box stays scoped to the probe.
    • The existing choice tasks judge supplied observations, and the existing sample tab is ungraded. Implement the editable probe and its evidence predicate as a small vision-domain task capability before authoring the opening content. Use the shared image editor/display where the supported controls fit. Supply reference and insufficient-evidence cases to the domain check. The phase succeeds when a learner can run and submit their own input on both desktop and phone.

    Final processor contract¶

    • One part, two forms, chosen on the card (engine/forms.js). Only the selected form's passed board runs.
    Form Ports Built in Accepts
    Dense image, kernel → out 1-2, upgraded in 1-3 and 1-4 any nonempty kernel, odd in each dimension
    Separable image, kx (1×kx row), ky (ky×1 column) → out 1-5 any two odd factors

    The separable form applies kx horizontally and then ky vertically; its result equals the dense form on the outer product ky·kx. Deciding whether a kernel factors, and writing the factors, is the caller's decision. No adapter factors kernels at run time. - All inputs are real single-channel grids. Convolution uses centred written coordinates and reads image(x-u,y-v) for a coefficient at offset (u,v). Values stay signed and unclipped. - outside is forwarded to the actual reads of the selected form. Supported values are zero, clamp, wrap and reflection including the edge. Each rule maps coordinates per axis, so the two forms agree under all four. The output is the same size as the input; applications that want valid output crop explicitly by the kernel radii. - Cost is the multiply count measured on the executed board, shown with output dimensions and the form. On a W×H image the expected counts are W·H·kx·ky (dense) and W·H·(kx+ky) (separable). A learner board may count fewer, for example by eliding zero terms; the display reports the measurement, not the formula.

    Domain capability, prototyped before 1-5 is authored: a learner-built part whose forms each run their own passed board, with forms gained along the course (revisions with by and forms). The logic domain does this for De Morgan's forms; the vision domain does not yet. The prototype must show that a board calls the selected form only, that its cost counts that form only, and that detail and Edit open the board of the selected form.

    Implementation sequence and gates¶

    1. Implement and exercise the opening probe as a standalone domain capability. Check distinguishing and insufficient inputs, including an impulse and a flat patch for gain, before adding it to 1-1.
    2. Prototype per-form passed boards for a learner-built part in the vision domain, with form selection, cost counting and detail views as above.
    3. Remove old 1-9, renumber, rearrange the chapter into the parallel layout, publish the dense form through 1-2 to 1-4, add the separable form in 1-5 and turn 1-6 to 1-9 into callers, all through the content operation set.
    4. Migrate chapter 2 consumers and retire superseded presets together with the dependent content changes. Keep playable intermediate commits by leaving a referenced preset available until this change lands.
    5. Run the Vision Lab content check, affected domain and circuit tests, and game build. Audit final capability unlocks, actual saved-board reuse in both forms, all sample inputs and outputs, and the dependency graph. Review the probe, both forms and the 2-4 result rows in desktop and phone views. Whether a player can explain the evidence and apply it in the next task needs a human session; blind play by an agent does not show this.
    6. Start the chapter 3 illumination route after the processor is stable. Before writing chapter 4 level content, demonstrate parameter storage, differentiation through a composed learner circuit, repeated updates and separation of training and validation data in a minimal prototype.
