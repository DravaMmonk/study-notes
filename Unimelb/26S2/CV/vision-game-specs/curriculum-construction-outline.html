    COMP90086: machines, parts and knowledge coverage¶

    Status: stages 0–2 implemented in Vision Lab, 2026-11-04; later chapters are planned in the chapter map. See the executable stage design and the stage review for actual tasks, contracts and verification limits. Capability labels in this outline are not executable level IDs.

    1. Priority and planning method¶

    Components and assembly are the core. Plan the machine first, then the capability ladder and reusable parts; cover as much course knowledge as possible through those constructions, applications, debugging cases and constraints. Only important knowledge that cannot be meaningfully covered by these mechanisms receives an additional lesson. The main path is not a lecture sequence with circuit exercises attached.

    Planning order:

    1. Define a concrete machine the player will make and its observable input/output contract.
    2. Work backwards to the capabilities and parts it needs, ending at a small useful primitive vocabulary.
    3. Arrange construction -> immediate use -> changed-case debugging -> encapsulation -> larger construction.
    4. Map lecture knowledge onto those actions. Improve the test material, parameter exposure, failure feedback or construction stages to cover missing facets before adding another level.
    5. Move a mechanism to where the machine first needs it. Lecture number records provenance, not play order.
    6. Create an extra lesson only for an important residual concept that does not fit the construction path.

    A level is worth keeping when the learner must understand a relationship to make a working construction. A part must earn its place through subsequent use. New pictures or numbers alone are tests. Copying a coordinate, learning an arithmetic button or choosing a named ready-made answer is not a complete level.

    Original lecture PDFs and transcripts establish what the knowledge means. Necessary prerequisites and scaffolding may be supplied. A novel scanner or retrieval scene can be on the main path when it combines learned course ideas; an unverified additional scientific claim must not be smuggled in as a required discovery.

    2. Primitive boundary: assist execution, preserve construction¶

    Supplied support Learner-owned construction
    Image/vector arithmetic, constants, numeric evaluation and visual inspection How information paths are combined to satisfy the visual specification
    Spatial shift or sample access, coordinate generation, fixed conventions and index/rounding helpers Neighbour selection, weighted spatial composition and interpolation contributions
    Pattern duplication, traversal/repetition, normalisation arithmetic and reductions The rule being repeated, its weights, its assumptions and the composition of rules
    Basic mathematical functions, linear-system solvers and automatic cost accounting Fourier projection, geometric relations, robust-estimation policy and efficiency decisions
    Parameter storage, differentiation, optimiser updates, random draws and training execution Model structure, objectives, data routing, training/evaluation protocol and sampling process
    A declared sensor, scene renderer or physical model A forward model or processing system where that relation is actually constructible

    Do not hand out complete Gaussian/Sobel filters, a bilinear sampler, an attention block, a matcher or an AP calculator before the player has built the corresponding core. These are earned abstractions. A shift is a reasonable starting spatial operation; a complete method hiding the assessed decomposition is not a convenience.

    Arithmetic evaluation does not need to be hand-written or calculated. Keeping multiplication and addition as parts is compatible with supplying their numerical results. Provide prerequisites, examples and accessible formulas; retain a real decomposition or design decision rather than a transcription exercise. Starter wiring may remove repetition but must not preassemble the target's decisive information path.

    A familiar mathematical helper gets a brief explanation where needed, not its own test. A repeated graph pattern can be cloned after its meaning has been acquired. The learner's actual passed graph is what an earned part runs. Parameter exposure, gradients, iteration and value shapes must be implemented before promising executable reuse.

    4. Opening: a small image machine with an immediate purpose¶

    Shared introduction¶

    Start in an inspection station with an observed image and a reference. State what a controlled defect means in this scene and what the requested output represents. Explain image coordinates, channel values and the first few operations alongside this problem. A camera is an available source of images, not an optics prerequisite. No isolated coordinate-copying, constant-setting, arithmetic drill or task-name quiz precedes construction.

    Short construction sequence¶

    Sequence Construction and observable problem Knowledge covered What is supplied / what must remain
    F1. Compare image evidence Build a reusable per-location comparison that exposes both darker and brighter changes across different images Values versus locations, corresponding cells/channels, signed difference and magnitude as necessary mathematical support Arithmetic/absolute-value evaluation; the player still combines observation and reference in the correct roles
    F2. Compare the same locations Reuse comparison after correcting a supplied, known spatial displacement Spatial movement versus brightness change, coordinate direction and correspondence The shift operation and displacement; do not introduce an image-registration algorithm here
    F3. A controlled inspection instrument Compose alignment, comparison and a supplied comparison/threshold primitive to produce the declared change evidence Image-shaped outputs, composition and the limits of a pixelwise inspection rule Numeric tolerance and comparison mechanics; no industrial calibration or object-recognition claim

    F1–F3 are a short opening sequence, not a demand for three separately padded levels. Combine simple steps as tasks and tests when they do not introduce a substantive new facet. Inversion, brightness and contrast examples are included only where they challenge this construction; they do not each require a separate level.

    The inspection problem has controlled images and a known displacement. Variable illumination is a later upgrade using learned light-invariant measurements, not an unexplained extra requirement at the beginning. Its comparator is reused for residual/error inspection. No one-use helper is packaged merely to advertise an unlock.

    5. Neighbourhood machine: the first substantial construction ladder¶

    Begin with a concrete failure of pointwise comparison: local nuisance and broad boundaries cannot be separated by looking at one reading alone. Establish the controlled signal/nuisance assumptions. The player extends the existing image machine rather than entering a new worksheet.

    Sequence Learner-owned construction Knowledge embedded in construction, use and debugging Later use
    N1. Combine neighbours Build a three-sample weighted line filter from spatial reads/shifts and arithmetic Locality, positional weights, gain and the change from one reading to a neighbourhood; normalisation arithmetic is automatic Reused along both axes and inside later local methods
    N2. Compose directions Reuse the acquired filter structure in perpendicular directions to make a 2D average Outer-product contributions, direction and the first concrete case of separability; automatic cost shows the saving A reusable separable composition, later tested with other weights
    N3. Generalise the local rule Build a reusable local-kernel structure, with repeated layout assistance rather than nine copies typed by hand Impulse response, weight placement, written kernel versus neighbourhood convention, convolution/correlation General kernels for smoothing, derivatives, sharpening, colour and convolutional layers
    N4. Make a smoother Use the player's kernel to construct Gaussian/average smoothing, exposing the relevant parameters Normalisation, point spread, sigma versus support, constant gain and fine-detail loss; Gaussian values/formula are available Scanner preprocessing, structure tensors and signal conditioning
    N5. Measure change Build signed differences, then compose perpendicular smoothing for Sobel; combine components into strength only when needed Direction, contrast sign, orthogonal smoothing and L5 vector meaning; supplied magnitude arithmetic does not choose the information path Scanner measurements, later Harris/descriptor evidence and illumination tests
    N6. Stop inventing border evidence Extend the player's filter/composition with explicit boundary behaviour and valid/same output domains Missing neighbours, valid output size, zero/wrap/replicate/reflect assumptions, artificial responses and equivalence limits Every later spatial operator that reaches an image edge
    N7. Use the machine in different ways Reuse the general kernel/composition for sharpening, directional variations and a changed factorable/non-factorable case Common-filter effects, separability limits, linearity and combined-kernel efficiency Meaningful practice of the same machine, not a new primitive per filter
    N8. Extend across channels Generalise the acquired weighted neighbourhood into spatial-and-channel aggregation Image shape, channel contributions and the role of multiple kernels; channel storage/looping is supplied A real prerequisite for a parameterised convolutional layer
    N9. Build the scanner response pipeline Compose the player's smoother, directional responses/strength and boundary handling Scale choices, signal path and the interaction of already learned methods on noise, fine text and broad page boundaries A concrete chapter payoff; later line fitting and geometry can upgrade the same scanner

    The table describes dependencies and practice facets, not nine mandatory serial levels. N1 is the common basis. Smoothing, change responses and boundary upgrades can branch after their actual prerequisites. N9 uses only the branches actually consumed by its reference and transfer cases. Colour aggregation is required course learning, but it need not be a fake prerequisite of a greyscale scanner; it leads naturally into the learned-network branch.

    The Gaussian, Sobel, kernel and separable operators are earned abstractions. Giving them as complete initial primitives and asking for a mode choice would remove this ladder. Conversely, normalisation fractions, repeated weight wiring, image traversal and hand-calculated output tables are not the ladder and are assisted.

    Kernel convention is exposed by asymmetric impulse material; separability by equivalence plus automatically accounted cost; boundary assumptions by meaningful image evidence. These are embedded in construction and practice instead of separate reading-comprehension gates. Compare composed filters over a common valid interior or an explicitly correct padded/cropped domain; ideal linear identities must not be falsely asserted at edges.

    7. Knowledge coverage audit and residual lessons¶

    Every course knowledge point receives one of the following planned coverage records. A mention or glossary link does not establish practice or mastery. Record the actual action, material, misconception and source.

    Coverage route Use it when Example
    Build The idea can be embodied in a reusable relationship or algorithm Weighted neighbourhood, attention, interpolation or IoU
    Use A constructed part can be put into a new meaningful role Smoothing before a change measurement; crop/sampling inside an aligner
    Debug/counterexample A wrong model or assumption becomes visible in a changed case Padding creates a false boundary; data leakage or duplicate detections changes evaluation
    Constraint/upgrade A theoretical property explains why an implementation can be improved Separable equivalence and cost, parameter sharing, residual identity paths
    Extra lesson Important knowledge cannot be meaningfully expressed by the construction path or the available model A physical focus/depth-of-field experiment after the camera module
    Reference Background or implementation detail needs explanation but no substantial practice File/API conventions, channel storage order, numeric format and interface operation

    Before allocating an extra lesson, try to expose the concept in an existing construction's parameters, input conditions, failure case or later upgrade. Do not force an arbitrary fact into wiring just to claim coverage, and do not change meaningful construction into a generic named-operator choice merely to count another point.

    Examples of placement decisions:

    • Image values/coordinates/channels are acquired while building and inspecting the opening machine. There is no preliminary coordinate worksheet.
    • Camera projection, intrinsics/extrinsics and field of view belong with the camera/depth machine, where they change the actual mapping and search. They do not gate image filtering.
    • Forward/inverse ambiguity can be exposed using two constructed forward models yielding the same observation in the light or geometry machine. If the required evidence cannot be presented there, create one focused extra experiment after its real prerequisite.
    • Nearest and bilinear interpolation belong to the first machine that genuinely needs source-image resampling, provisionally the planar aligner. The general rule is first need, not a permanently fixed chapter number.
    • Aperture/depth of field is a candidate extra physical-model experiment. Its model and explanatory image must be adequate; a fact-only quiz or a slider with a green target is insufficient.
    • Train/validation/test, regularisation, transfer and robustness should be exercised in the learner's training controller and its new-data tests before considering a separate experimental lesson.
    • Occlusion/CAM-style computations and attribution checks should extend the player's own model where possible. True Grad-CAM needs access to channel-wise feature gradients; a linear toy heatmap is not equivalent.
    • References to a lecture, transcript, assignment or Workshop remain explicit. Existing prototype tricks such as repeated box smoothing, photometric stereo or a particular sampling puzzle are not automatically required course facts. Verify their sources and role; use them only as justified construction support or labelled extensions.

    8. Evidence, scope and implementation requirements¶

    Sources are the original COMP90086 lecture PDFs/transcripts under ../lecture/ and ../transcript/. The notes below navigate those sources, not a claim that all final tasks have been audited:

    L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, L7, L8, L9, L10, L11, L12, L13, L14, L15, L16, L17, L19, L20, L21, L22.

    Original headings and available learning outcomes were inspected for the complete course in the outline work. Early source anchors include L2 p5/p22–26 for images, p34–40 for resampling; L3 p6–18 for locality/weights/convention, p21–35 for common filters/colour/support, p37–40 for properties/separability, p41–45 for borders; and L5 p39–42 for gradient meaning. Page numbers are PDF indices. Later tasks still need exact source and prerequisite audits.

    Before implementation:

    1. Specify every machine's inputs/outputs, primitive boundary, part graph and actual next use. Draw real branches.
    2. Audit course coverage through build/use/debug/constraint records. Identify important unplaced knowledge and justify each extra lesson. Do not infer coverage from a title, test pass or diagram.
    3. Ensure mobile/desktop interaction exposes original values, contributions and failures at a compact size. Provide repeat-layout/calculation assistance and keep result drawers initially closed. Do not hide the central relation inside the tool supplied to make a puzzle easier.
    4. Verify that saved constructions can be parameterised, iterated, differentiated and traced as later machines require. A missing capability is domain work, not a reason to replace construction with instruction text.
    5. Validate mathematical cases and characteristic wrong methods, source/prerequisite access, actual interactions and fresh-player learning separately. Automated checks cannot establish understanding or enjoyment.

    The executable Vision Lab now covers stages 0–2 through its own blueprint, task data and domain implementations. Later stages remain machine-level proposals and require the source, interface and interaction work listed above.
