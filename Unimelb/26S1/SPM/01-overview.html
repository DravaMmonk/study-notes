    1 Overview¶

    Business-Driven IT

    What is a Project¶

    • Period: Temporary endeavour (with a start & end)
    • Team: Cross-functional; Size Variable
    • Uncertainty: Deal with unknown
    • Product: Unique; Introduce Change

    Why do organisations use Projects¶

    • When creating something new
    • Provides alignment of key activities & deliveries & how to prioritise them
    • Easily structured and formalised (for business)
    • Easily control of limit resources
    • Establish ownership, agreement and accountability

    Project Management = Trade-off¶

    • Project Management Triangle: resources, scope and time
    • PMI 6 constraints model: scope, time, cost, quality, risk, resources

    Key Responsibilities of the Project Manager¶

    Initialisation Maintaining
    Project Planning Controlling
    Team Organising Leading

    Project Charter - Beginning of the project¶

    A set of documents which tells: - Project Purpose - Business Case - Project Objectives - Scope (Included & Excluded) - Schedule - Roles & Responsibilities - Budget Estimations - Major Risks - Success Criteria

    The project begins when the Project Charter is permitted by sponsors

    Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Models¶

    Formal SDLC¶

    • Waterfall
    • Incremental
    • Spiral

    When to choose Formal Models?
    - Customer knows what they want at the start - Stable, precise and known requirements - Change is not expected - Mature technologies and tools

    Agile SDLC¶

    • Scrum
    • Kanban

    When to choose Agile Models?
    - Customer gives time to project - Requirements continue to emerge - Change is welcome

    When to choose Hybrid Models?
    - Client has a prescriptive model established
